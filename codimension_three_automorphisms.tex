\begin{abstract}
Let $\pi\colon Y\to X$ be a finite composition of blow-ups of smooth complex
projective varieties along smooth centres of codimension at least three.
We prove that a finite-index subgroup of $\Aut(Y)$ descends faithfully to
$\Aut(X)$. In particular, such blow-ups cannot turn a finite automorphism
group into an infinite one. For a blow-up with connected nonempty centre,
the numerical pullback space is a hyperplane on which the Hessian
determinant of the top intersection form vanishes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

All varieties are integral and defined over $\C$, and $\Aut(X)$ denotes
the group of biregular automorphisms of $X$. Lesieutre and Litt
\cite[Theorem~1.6]{LL} prove a uniform descent theorem for certain sequences
of smooth blow-ups. For a centre of dimension $r$ in an $n$-fold, their
hypotheses are $2r+3\leq n$, or $r+3\leq n$ together with polyhedrality of
the nef cone of the exceptional divisor. They ask whether blow-ups along
smooth centres of codimension at least three can turn a finite automorphism
group into an infinite one \cite[Question~1.8]{LL}. We give a negative
answer in the smooth complex-projective case.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let
\[
Y=X_m\xrightarrow{\pi_m}X_{m-1}\longrightarrow\cdots
\xrightarrow{\pi_1}X_0=X
\]
be a finite sequence of smooth projective varieties, where each $\pi_i$
is the ordinary blow-up of $X_{i-1}$ along a smooth closed centre, every connected
component of which has codimension at least three. Put
$\pi=\pi_1\circ\cdots\circ\pi_m$.
There is a finite-index subgroup $G\leq\Aut(Y)$ such that every $g\in G$
admits a unique $\bar g\in\Aut(X)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:descent}
\pi\circ g=\bar g\circ\pi.
\end{equation}
The homomorphism $G\to\Aut(X)$, $g\mapsto\bar g$, is injective.
Consequently, if $\Aut(X)$ is finite, then $\Aut(Y)$ is finite.
\end{theorem}

We use the top intersection form on numerical divisor classes. Its Hessian
determinant is nonzero by the Hodge index theorem. A blow-up along a
connected nonempty centre of codimension at least three determines a
hyperplane on which this determinant vanishes.
There are only finitely many such hyperplanes, so a finite-index subgroup
preserves the numerical data of the blow-down. The remaining step is to
recover the actual fibres from those data.

\section{The intersection discriminant}

Let $Z$ be a smooth projective $n$-fold, with $n\geq3$, and set
$V=\NOne{Z}$ and $\rho=\rho(Z)=\dim_{\R}V$.
Here $\NOne{Z}$ is the space of real Cartier divisors modulo numerical
equivalence. Choose a basis $D_1,\ldots,D_\rho$ of $V$. For $h,u,v\in V$,
define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discriminant}
q_h(u,v)=\int_Zh^{n-2}uv,\qquad
M_Z(h)=\bigl(q_h(D_i,D_j)\bigr)_{i,j},\qquad
\Delta_Z(h)=\det M_Z(h).
\end{equation}
The integrals denote intersection numbers. If
$h=\sum_i x_iD_i$ and $P_Z(x)=\int_Zh^n$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hessian}
\frac{\partial^2P_Z}{\partial x_i\partial x_j}
=n(n-1)q_h(D_i,D_j),\qquad
\det\Hess P_Z=\bigl(n(n-1)\bigr)^\rho\Delta_Z.
\end{equation}
Changing the basis multiplies $\Delta_Z$ by a nonzero square, so its zero
set is intrinsic.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:finite-hyperplanes}
The polynomial $\Delta_Z$ is nonzero and homogeneous of degree
$d_Z=(n-2)\rho$. Its zero set is preserved by pullback under automorphisms.
It contains at most $d_Z$ distinct real linear hyperplanes. Each of these
hyperplanes therefore has a stabilizer of index at most $d_Z$ in $\Aut(Z)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a very ample divisor class $a$.
The degree-two Hodge--Riemann bilinear relations
\cite[Theorem~7.3.4(b),(c)]{deCataldo} say that
$\alpha\mapsto\int_Z\alpha^2a^{n-2}$ is negative definite on the real
$(1,1)$-classes satisfying $\int_Z\alpha a^{n-1}=0$.
Numerical and real cohomological equivalence agree for divisors: a
numerically trivial real divisor has both of these intersection numbers
zero, so its Chern class vanishes by this negative definiteness; the
converse follows from the cohomological interpretation of intersection.
We may thus regard $V$ as the subspace of real $(1,1)$-classes spanned by
divisor classes. The orthogonal decomposition
\[
V=\R a\oplus\left\{u\in V:\int_Zu a^{n-1}=0\right\}
\]
has one positive direction, since $a^n>0$, and a negative definite
complement. Hence $q_a$ has signature $(1,\rho-1)$ and
$\Delta_Z(a)\ne0$.
The entries in \eqref{eq:discriminant} are homogeneous of degree $n-2$, which gives
the asserted degree.

For $g\in\Aut(Z)$, let $T$ be the matrix of $g^*$ in the chosen basis.
Invariance of intersection numbers gives
\[
q_{g^*h}(g^*u,g^*v)=q_h(u,v),\qquad
T^{\mathsf t}M_Z(g^*h)T=M_Z(h).
\]
Taking determinants yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covariance}
\Delta_Z(g^*h)=(\det T)^{-2}\Delta_Z(h).
\end{equation}
Finally, a polynomial vanishing on a real hyperplane is divisible by its
defining linear form: in coordinates where the hyperplane is $x_\rho=0$,
the constant coefficient in $x_\rho$ vanishes identically.
A nonzero polynomial of degree $d_Z$ has at most $d_Z$ linear factors
up to nonzero scalars. Pullback permutes the corresponding hyperplanes, proving the
index bound. We use the right action $W\cdot g=g^*W$ on hyperplanes.
\end{proof}

\section{Blow-ups and descent}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:single-step}
Let $\pi\colon Y=\Bl_S X\to X$ be the blow-up of a smooth projective
$n$-fold along a nonempty connected smooth centre of codimension $c\geq3$.
The stabilizer of $\pi^*\NOne{X}$ in $\Aut(Y)$ has index at most
$(n-2)\rho(Y)$ and descends faithfully to $\Aut(X)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $E$ be the exceptional divisor, and write $p\colon E\to S$ and
$i\colon E\hookrightarrow Y$ for the natural maps. The morphism $p$ is a
$\PP^{c-1}$-bundle, and $\mathcal O_E(E)$ restricts to
$\mathcal O_{\PP^{c-1}}(-1)$ on its fibres \cite[Tag~0FUT]{Stacks}.
We have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomposition}
\NOne{Y}=\pi^*\NOne{X}\oplus\R[E].
\end{equation}
Indeed, $E$ is prime. Every other prime divisor $F$ on $Y$ is the strict
transform of a Cartier divisor $D=\pi(F)$ on $X$, so
$F=\pi^*D-a_FE$ for some integer $a_F$. Thus pullbacks and $E$ span
$\NOne{Y}$.
Pullback is numerically injective: for an integral curve $B\subset X$,
take the reduced closure of a closed point of the generic fibre of
$\pi^{-1}(B)\to B$. This is an integral curve $C$ dominating $B$ with
some finite degree $e>0$, and
$(\pi^*D)\cdot C=e(D\cdot B)$.
For a line $\ell$ in a fibre of $p$, one has
$(\pi^*D)\cdot\ell=0$ and $E\cdot\ell=-1$.
This proves the direct sum in \eqref{eq:decomposition}.

Put $W=\pi^*\NOne{X}$, a hyperplane in $\NOne{Y}$.
For $h=\pi^*a\in W$ and $u\in\NOne{Y}$, restriction to $E$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exceptional-kernel}
q_h(E,u)=\int_Y(\pi^*a)^{n-2}Eu
=\int_Ep^*(a|_S)^{n-2}i^*u=0.
\end{equation}
The last equality holds because
$\dim S=n-c<n-2$. Since $[E]\ne0$, the form $q_h$ is degenerate for
every $h\in W$. Thus $W\subset\{\Delta_Y=0\}$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-hyperplanes} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-index}
G_\pi=\{g\in\Aut(Y):g^*W=W\},\qquad
[\Aut(Y):G_\pi]\leq(n-2)\rho(Y).
\end{equation}

We next prove regular descent. Choose an ample divisor $A$ on $X$.
If $g\in G_\pi$ and $C\subset Y$ is an integral curve contracted by
$\pi$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contracted-curves}
A\cdot\pi_*g_*[C]=(g^*\pi^*A)\cdot C=0,
\end{equation}
since $g^*\pi^*A\in W$.
If $\pi(g(C))$ were a curve, the left side would be positive.
Hence $\pi g$ contracts every such $C$.
Each nontrivial closed fibre of $\pi$ is $\PP^{c-1}$, whose closed points are
joined by lines. Therefore $\pi g$ is constant on every closed fibre.

Let $\Gamma$ be the reduced closed image of
$(\pi,\pi g)\colon Y\to X\times X$.
It is integral, and its first projection $q\colon\Gamma\to X$ is proper.
Every closed fibre of $q$ has just one closed point and is therefore
zero-dimensional. Upper semicontinuity of fibre dimension for a proper
morphism \cite[Tag~0D4I]{Stacks} implies that all its fibres are zero-dimensional: a nonempty
closed locus of positive-dimensional fibres would contain a closed point.
Thus $q$ is quasi-finite and hence finite \cite[Tag~02LS]{Stacks}.
Over $X\setminus S$, the image is the graph of $\pi g\pi^{-1}$, so $q$
is birational. Since $X$ is normal, $q$ is an isomorphism
\cite[Tag~0AB1]{Stacks}. The second projection defines a morphism
$\bar g\colon X\to X$ satisfying \eqref{eq:descent}.
Applying the same argument to $g^{-1}$ supplies its inverse.
Uniqueness and compatibility with composition follow from
\eqref{eq:descent} and the surjectivity of $\pi$.
An element descending to the identity fixes $\pi^{-1}(X\setminus S)$
pointwise and hence is the identity on $Y$. This proves faithfulness.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
A smooth centre has finitely many disjoint connected components.
Blowing up their union is the same as blowing them up successively:
on the open set obtained by removing all but one component, only that
component contributes, and these identifications agree on overlaps.
The remaining components lie in the isomorphism locus of each preceding
step, so their codimensions do not change. Refine the sequence in this
way, omit empty centres, and relabel its terms $X_0,\ldots,X_m$.
If no steps remain, $\pi$ is an isomorphism and the assertion follows.
Otherwise $n=\dim X\geq3$.

For each step, Proposition~\ref{prop:single-step} gives a finite-index
subgroup $G_i\leq\Aut(X_i)$ and an injective descent homomorphism
$\delta_i\colon G_i\to\Aut(X_{i-1})$.
Set $H_0=\Aut(X_0)$ and $H_i=\delta_i^{-1}(H_{i-1})$.
The elementary coset inequality gives
\[
[G_i:H_i]
=[\delta_i(G_i):\delta_i(G_i)\cap H_{i-1}]
\leq[\Aut(X_{i-1}):H_{i-1}].
\]
Induction shows that $H_i$ has finite index in $\Aut(X_i)$ and descends
faithfully through the first $i$ steps. Take $G=H_m$.
If $\Aut(X)$ is finite, then $G$ is finite, and its finite index makes
$\Aut(Y)$ finite.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:index}
For the refined nonempty sequence, the proof gives the explicit bound
\[
[\Aut(Y):G]\leq\prod_{i=1}^m(n-2)\rho(X_i)
=(n-2)^m\prod_{i=1}^m\bigl(\rho(X)+i\bigr),
\]
where $m$ now counts the connected nonempty centres. The last equality
uses \eqref{eq:decomposition} at each step.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:codimension-two}
The codimension condition enters in \eqref{eq:exceptional-kernel}.
If $c=2$ and $a$ is very ample on $X$, put
$\xi=c_1(\mathcal O_E(-E))$. Since $p\colon E\to S$ has fibre $\PP^1$
and $p_*\xi=1$, the same calculation instead gives
\[
q_{\pi^*a}(E,E)=-\int_Ep^*(a|_S)^{n-2}\xi
=-\int_S(a|_S)^{n-2}<0.
\]
The mixed pairings vanish because they restrict to $n-1$ divisor factors
on $S$. On the pullback space the form is the nondegenerate form $q_a$
of $X$. Consequently
$\Delta_Y(\pi^*a)\ne0$ in this case.
Uniform descent can indeed fail in codimension two
\cite[Example~2.5]{LL}.
\end{remark}
